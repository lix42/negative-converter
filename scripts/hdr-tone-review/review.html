max does not discriminate here, and that is itself a finding. On these frames the shouldered and unbounded renders land at 4.87x and 4.79x — both near the 4.926 ceiling, and identical on all four frames, because GainMapMax is a single extremum that any near-asymptotic highlight reaches.

at top code is the metric. It is the share of the gain map pinned at its maximum code — the flat blob, in numbers. The shoulder fuses 6.6–15.2% of the frame onto one code; the unbounded tone 0.26–0.61%, a 10–25x reduction. For default a high share just means the map is uniformly inert.

What to judge

  1. Does s0-reinhard read as HDR at all? Highlights should extend past paper white with gradation, not glow uniformly.
  2. s0-shoulder vs s0-reinhard — the main comparison. Measured: the shoulder pins 6.6–15.2% of each frame at one gain code, the unbounded tone 0.26–0.61%. So look at the brightest areas and ask whether the shoulder's are a uniform wash where the unbounded one holds shape. This is the whole argument for the operator, and the eye has overruled these metrics twice before — so it decides.
  3. The one-stop question. Reinhard maps diffuse white to ~0.5 by construction: measured 0.24 stops down at middle grey but a full 1.00 stop at diffuse white. That is why every tone-mapped render looked darker in the SDR review — it is the operator, not the blue cast. In HDR the headroom may absorb it, or the image may just read dark. This is a rendering-intent call and it is yours.
  4. default is the shipped baseline and its gain map is inert, so it is effectively the SDR render. Hold space to A/B against it.
  5. Blue cast is out of scope — deferred to the colour work by prior decision.
